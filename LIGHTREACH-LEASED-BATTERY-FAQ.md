# Can GridPay work with my leased battery? (Palmetto LightReach and similar leases)

**Audience: Star A.I. (Tavus) and Zendesk AI agent. Customer-facing tone. Verified against a LightReach Arizona Battery Lease sample, 2026-08-11. Applies generally to LightReach and similar third-party-owned (TPO) battery leases; exact terms vary by contract and state.**

## The short answer agents should give
Yes, GridPay can help you, and no, we will never touch a leased battery's settings. If your battery is leased (LightReach, Solrite, or a similar provider owns it), the leasing company owns and operates the battery, and your lease prohibits anyone from modifying it. What GridPay works with is YOUR side of the meter: your utility data, your electricity plan, and your bill. That does not belong to the leasing company.

## What GridPay CAN do for leased-battery customers
1. Read your utility meter data (with your permission) and verify exactly what your home exports and imports.
2. Review your electricity plan and find mismatches that cost you money. Your plan choice is yours, not the lessor's.
3. Produce a battery earnings report: what the leased battery is actually doing for your bill. The LightReach lease itself states that compensation from grid programs "could be paid to you, if any" and that the company's management of the system "could reduce any such compensation." Nobody on the homeowner's side is measuring that. GridPay measures it.
4. Advocate for you with the numbers if the battery is configured against your interests.

## What GridPay will NOT do on a leased battery, ever
1. Change battery settings or operating modes. The lease requires the owner's explicit authorization and violating it is a breach with real consequences for the homeowner.
2. Enroll a leased battery in any VPP or grid program. The lessor holds those rights.
3. Promise specific dollar amounts before seeing real meter data.

## If the customer OWNS their battery (cash or loan)
Everything changes: full GridPay service applies, including optimization and grid program enrollment. Ownership is the first question to ask. Loan-financed batteries count as owned.

## Key facts from the LightReach AZ lease (for agent accuracy, do not quote as legal advice)
- LightReach owns the battery system "for all purposes, including any data."
- Customer cannot remove or modify the system without LightReach authorization.
- Customer pre-authorizes LightReach to use stored energy for demand response, managed by a vendor of LightReach's choosing.
- Tax credits and incentives belong exclusively to LightReach. Rebates available only to the customer stay with the customer, and the customer's own utility bill savings and export credits are the customer's.
- Two battery types appear: an Energy Arbitrage Battery (no backup power in an outage) and a backup battery. Agents should never promise outage backup without knowing which type the customer has.

## Tone guidance
Never disparage the leasing company. Never call any lease a scam. Never state that the customer is losing money before their meter data proves anything. The honest line: "Your lease means the battery belongs to them, but your meter and your bill belong to you. We work for your side."

## Escalation
Questions about breaking, exiting, or disputing a lease go to a human (Devan), not to an AI answer. Agents may say a teammate will follow up.

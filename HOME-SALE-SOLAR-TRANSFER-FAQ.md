# Buying or selling a home with solar or a battery: how it works with GridPay

**Audience: Star A.I. (Tavus) and Zendesk AI agent. Customer-facing. Synthesized 2026-08-11 from Sunrun's own seller and realtor guides plus GridPay's model. No customer names or addresses appear here. When a caller asks about their specific agreement, ask them to have it handy and give general guidance, then offer human follow-up for the exact terms.**

## The one thing that determines everything: who owns the system
Before anything else, find out how the solar or battery was paid for. It decides how a sale works and what GridPay can do.
- **Owned (cash or loan):** the homeowner owns the equipment. It usually conveys with the house like any other fixture. A loan can be paid off at closing or, sometimes, assumed by the buyer. GridPay can fully serve an owned system, before or after a sale.
- **Leased or Power Purchase Agreement (PPA):** a company (Sunrun, LightReach, and others) owns the equipment and the homeowner pays monthly. The agreement must be TRANSFERRED to the buyer, or bought out, at sale. GridPay works the meter and bill side only on leased systems; we never change a leased system's settings.

## Selling a home that has solar or a battery
1. Tell the provider as soon as the home is under contract. Transfers take time and can hold up closing if they start late.
2. Know your agreement type. Owned conveys or gets paid off. Leases and PPAs transfer to a qualifying buyer, and every agreement type can generally be transferred.
3. Make the solar or battery agreement part of the purchase contract as a contingency, so the buyer formally agrees to take over the service and its payment terms.
4. Use professionals familiar with rooftop solar: agent, lender, and title company. It prevents the deal from stalling.
5. Solar can help. The Department of Energy has found homes with solar often sell faster, and studies show a resale premium. That is a selling point, not a problem to hide.

## Buying a home that has solar or a battery
1. Ask which company owns the system and how it was paid for. This is the single most important question.
2. If leased or PPA, read the agreement: monthly payment, annual escalator, remaining term, and what transfers to you. You are agreeing to those payments.
3. Ask about backup. Some batteries provide outage backup and some (arbitrage-only batteries) do not. Do not assume.
4. Ask what the system actually produces and what it saves. GridPay can verify real export and consumption from the utility meter, so the numbers are measured, not promised.
5. Understand tax credits and incentives. On most leases those belong to the leasing company, not to you.

## Difference between the common companies and structures
- **Owned system, any installer:** homeowner keeps all value and all incentives. Fullest GridPay play.
- **Sunrun (lease, PPA, or loan):** transferable at sale; agreement type sets who keeps incentives. On a Sunrun lease or PPA, the provider retains system rights; on a Sunrun loan the homeowner owns the equipment.
- **LightReach / Palmetto (lease):** the provider owns the battery and its data, controls demand-response participation through a vendor of its choosing, and holds the tax credits. Homeowner cannot modify the system. See the leased-battery FAQ for detail.
- **Solrite and similar TPO leases:** same pattern as LightReach; provider-owned and provider-controlled.

## Where GridPay fits, on either side of a sale
GridPay is the meter-and-bill layer, not the hardware. That means:
- On an OWNED system, GridPay can optimize the plan, verify exports, and enroll eligible batteries so the homeowner earns from the system.
- On a LEASED system, GridPay reads the utility meter, verifies what the system is doing for the bill, and advocates for the homeowner. GridPay never changes a leased system's settings.
- A new buyer inheriting a solar home can use GridPay to understand and monetize whatever they are taking over, once ownership or a valid transfer is in place.

## Tone and safety guidance for agents
- Never disparage any solar company or call any agreement a scam.
- Never quote a specific customer's contract terms, payment, or address from memory. Ask them to open their own agreement, or take their info for a human to answer exactly.
- Never promise a specific home-value increase or a specific dollar saving before real meter data.
- Lease-exit, buyout, dispute, or transfer-paperwork questions go to a human (Devan), not to an AI answer.

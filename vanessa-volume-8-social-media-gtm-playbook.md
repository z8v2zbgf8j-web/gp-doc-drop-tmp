The keyword research for hyper-local terms like "St. George hospital" or "urgent care St. George" comes back with almost no measurable national search volume. That is not a red flag. It is how local search works. Content should win on local relevance and community trust, not national search volume.

## Five trust pillars

Every post should fall under one of these:

1. **Behind the badge.** Show the facility, the equipment, the process. The institution becomes a place and a team, not a logo. No names, no patients, just the how.
2. **Plain-language education.** Explain a symptom or a procedure the way you would tell a neighbor, not a textbook. This is the highest-performing format.
3. **Community orientation.** New to St. George, what snowbirds need to know, local geography tied to health. A regional hospital beats national health channels here every time.
4. **Patient empowerment.** Questions to ask, how to choose a doctor, urgent care versus ER. Hand control back to the viewer. That is the direct antidote to distrust.
5. **Seasonal and local.** Desert heat, high-altitude sun, winter visitors, red-rock recreation injuries. Proof you are in the community, not just serving it.

**The rule that keeps it clean:** lead with the outcome for the patient, never the institution. Use exact numbers. Never promise a cure or a guarantee. That line separates trustworthy from salesy, and it is the whole point of this rebuild.

## Title formulas

National health channels run on fear. This channel runs on: we will explain it so you do not have to be afraid.

1. **Stop / Don't / Never + specific behavior + why**
   Example: "Never ignore these 3 symptoms after a hike" — specific, not sensational.
2. **The [specific thing] they don't explain**
   Example: "What a bill actually covers, explained" — demystification, not fear.
3. **A [credential] explains [common fear]**
   Example: "An ER nurse explains what actually happens when you arrive."
4. **Before you [common action]**
   Example: "Before you Google your symptoms, watch this."

## Thumbnail rules

- A calm, real face and one bold short phrase. The trust signal is a human, not a stock-photo grin.
- High contrast, three words maximum, over a relevant scene like a trail or the desert.
- Show the symptom or the object, never a frightened patient.
- No red-alarm clutter. That is the national playbook. This one is clean and credible.

## 90-day publishing cadence

Consistency reads as reliability, which is what a trust rebuild needs. Start lighter than feels necessary, and hold the rhythm before scaling it up.

| Weeks | Long-form | Shorts / Reels | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | 1/wk — Behind the badge | 3/wk — cut-downs plus 2 quick educational | Establish the rhythm, prove you are here to explain |
| 5–8 | 1/wk — Education, symptom explains | 4/wk — one original hook plus 3 repurposed | Build a searchable library and a daily presence |
| 9–12 | 1/wk — Empowerment, insurance | 4–5/wk — season into heat, skin, snowbird content | Peak on the seasonal topics that convert locally |

**The one thing to hold firm on:** the cadence matters less than the promise. Pick a rhythm you can sustain for all ninety days without missing a week. A missed week during a trust rebuild reads as unreliability, which is the exact wound you are healing.

## Two script formulas

One earns trust through explanation. One earns local relevance. The hospital's unowned lane is the overlap of both.

### The medical explainer, short-form

- **Hook (0–4s):** "This is [surprising but routine thing]" or "I just learned [surprising fact]." Lead with the thing, not yourself.
- **Stakes (4–10s):** One sentence on why the viewer should care.
- **Mechanism (10–35s):** How it actually works, step by step, with one plain analogy. This is the trust core.
- **Specificity (35–50s):** One exact number or a "we do this today" reveal.
- **Payoff:** Land the takeaway. No sales pitch, no subscribe ask.

### The local authority, short-form

- **Hook (0–6s):** "[Place] is [shifting] right now." Name the trend, not the channel.
- **Data (6–30s):** Three to five concrete, local numbers.
- **Local pivot (30–45s):** "For people in St. George, this means …"
- **Verdict (45–58s):** "This isn't just X. It's Y." A calibrated, non-alarmist read.
- **Close:** "Follow for updates before the next shift hits."

**The combined formula nobody else owns:** "This is actually routine in our ER" (explainer hook) × "For people in Southern Utah" (local pivot) = "This is routine in our ER, here's what it means for you in the desert." That sentence is the whole strategy.

## Social / NPM rollout objective

The operating objective is a 90-day trust rebuild of the hospital's public social presence, directed by Vanessa as a manager, not executed by her by hand.

How to coach reps and managers through the rollout:

- Assign one pillar and one topic at a time. Do not dump the whole list.
- Hold the cadence promise. A missed week is worse than a lighter week.
- Review drafts against the clean-content rule: patient outcome first, exact numbers, no cure/guarantee, no fear words.
- She is the editor-in-chief. The assistant drafts. The people with the accounts post.
- When she reports numbers ("this week's short got 4K views"), acknowledge them, note the trend in plain language, and suggest which pillar or topic to lean into next. The assistant is her log and sounding board, not a live dashboard. It only knows numbers she told it.

### Good social behavior versus gaming a score

Good:

- A post that clearly sits in one pillar
- A hook that explains instead of scares
- Exact numbers she actually has
- A sustainable weekly rhythm
- Coaching a rep to rewrite a draft so a neighbor would trust it

Gaming:

- Posting junk volume to hit a quota
- Fear hooks, red-alarm thumbnails, "miracle / cure / guaranteed" language
- Vague claims instead of exact numbers
- Missing a week, then dumping five posts
- Treating views or a score as the point instead of rebuilt trust

If someone asks how to "hit the number," steer them back to the pillar, the topic, and the promise they can keep.

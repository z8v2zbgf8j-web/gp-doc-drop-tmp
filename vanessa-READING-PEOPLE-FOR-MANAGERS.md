# Reading people, for a manager (hiring, interviewing, and leading a team)

**Vanessa's Manager Agent knowledge. Repurposes the 16-type real-time recognition model and NEPQ questioning from selling to INTERVIEWING and MANAGING. This is a communication and coaching aid, never a hiring pass or fail. Every read is a hypothesis to test in conversation, not a verdict, and never a reason to reject a candidate on type.**

## What to listen for in the first few minutes (of an interview or a conversation)
- Asks about numbers, structure, expectations up front: analytical, process types (ISTJ, INTJ, ESTJ, ESFJ). Give them clear structure and specifics.
- Asks about the team, the people, the culture: people-first types (ESFJ, ENFJ, ISFJ). Talk about who they will work with and how the team supports each other.
- Wants to understand the why and the mission: meaning-driven (INFJ, INFP, ENFP). Connect the role to purpose and impact.
- Wants to show they can do it now, action first: doers (ESTP, ISTP, ESTJ). Give them a concrete task or scenario.
- Asks lots of questions before committing: careful, thorough (ISTJ, ISFJ, INTP). Reassure them there is a right, proven way and walk it step by step.
- Warms up fast, talks quickly, high energy: enthusiasm-driven (ENFP, ESFP, ENTP). Match their energy, then anchor them with specifics.

## Interview questions by what you are trying to learn (NEPQ, reframed)
- Situation: "Walk me through a normal day in your last role. What did you own?"
- Problem: "Where did things tend to break down or get stuck for you?"
- Implication: "When that happened, what did it cost the team or the patients you served?"
- Values / fit: "What kind of team makes you do your best work?"
- Follow-through: "Tell me about a time you had too much on your plate. What did you actually do?"
Let them talk. The type read comes from HOW they answer, not just what they say.

## Using type to LEAD, not to screen out
- With a structure-first report: give clear steps, written expectations, and advance notice of change.
- With a people-first report: recognize them in front of others, frame tasks around who they help.
- With a meaning-first report: connect their work to the bigger why.
- With a doer: give autonomy and a concrete target, not a long meeting.
- With a careful, thorough report: give them the proven method and time to prepare.

## Hard line (must state if asked to "screen out" by type)
Type is a tool to understand and communicate with a person, not a filter to reject them. Using a personality read to decide who to hire or fire is not fair and can be legally risky. Use it to prepare better questions and to lead people well. Judge candidates on job-relevant evidence.
